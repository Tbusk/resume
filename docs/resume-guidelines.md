# Resume Guidelines

Apply these rules when reviewing or editing a resume. Prefer succinct, skimmable, ATS-parsable content. Treat the resume
as a ~30-second elevator pitch on paper.

## General Formatting

- Keep the resume to one page unless the candidate has 10+ years of experience. Rule of thumb: one page per decade of
  experience. A second page is only justified when the candidate can reasonably be considered for senior/staff roles and
  has enough relevant content.
- If a second page is used, put the most important content on the first page.
- Prefer a single-column layout.
- Avoid icons, images, and graphics.
- Do not indent sections and bullet points beyond the indent that bullets already create.
- Use bullet points, not paragraphs.
- Use one modern, easy-to-read font throughout: Calibri, Bitstream Charter, Carlito, Arial, Lato, or Helvetica.
- Use black text (not thin, not grey). Font size at least 10.5. Ensure sufficient contrast on a white background and
  that it remains readable in greyscale.
- Do not justify text; justified text creates uneven word spacing.
- Ensure enough whitespace. Minimum margins 0.5 inches is preferred; never below 0.4 inches. Minimum line spacing around
  1.07. No cramming.
- Avoid excessive italicization, bolding, and ALL CAPS. If emphasis is used, use only one style at a time and sparingly.
  Do not italicize body text for readability. Do not bold keywords inside bullet points.
- Separate sections clearly with consistent whitespace between sections, jobs, and projects.
- Ensure no spelling or grammar mistakes. Capitalize only proper nouns. Typos signal poor attention to detail.
- Spell out obscure abbreviations on first use (first letters of each word typically not capitalized unless they are
  proper nouns), e.g. scanning tunneling microscope (STM). Common industry terms may stay abbreviated after first
  expansion when clear from context.
- Prefer digits over spelled-out numbers: 8, not eight.
- Avoid apostrophes, ampersands, and slashes where possible.
- Avoid excess adjectives and adverbs (e.g. excellent, innovative, expert, revolutionary, disruptive, creatively,
  diligently, meticulously, strategically, successfully, independently).
- URLs must be clean: no tracking prefixes, no https:// or www. prefixes.

## Layout and Structure

- Contact header is always topmost.
- Section order depends on career stage:
    - Graduated with full-time experience: Work Experience, then Skills, then Education; or Skills, then Work
      Experience, then Education.
    - Student or new grad with limited work experience: Education, then Work Experience, then Skills; insert Projects
      before Skills when work experience is thin.
    - No technical work experience: Education, then Projects, then Work Experience, then Skills.
    - No work experience at all: Education, then Projects, then Volunteer/Extracurriculars, then Skills.
- Do not include a summary/profile unless the candidate is senior/staff or above, changing careers, or addressing an
  unemployment gap / return to workforce.
- Do not include a references section.
- List positions, projects, and education in reverse chronological order. Older items get less content than recent ones.
- Put critical, relevant details toward the top and left so they are more likely to be read.
- Name sections simply: Work Experience or Experience; Skills; Projects; Education. Avoid names like Professional
  Experience, Technical Skills, Selected Projects, etc.

## Dates

- Use Present for ongoing roles/projects, not Current, Now, or Ongoing.
- If months are included, use them consistently. Do not mix seasons or semesters (March 2022, not Winter 2022).
- Do not include specific days.
- Do not abbreviate years (2023, not '23).
- Do not use numeric month abbreviations like 9/2013.
- Month abbreviations, if used, follow: Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr, May, June, July, Aug, Sept, Oct, Nov, Dec. No periods after
  abbreviations.
- Date ranges use en dashes with spaces: Mar 2012 – Mar 2022. Not hyphens or the word "to".
- Right-align dates to the right margin.
- Bullet text must not extend further right than the dates.

## Contact Information

- Include name and exactly one email address.
- Optional when relevant near the name: security clearance (e.g. Top Secret / SCI eligible with CI Polygraph);
  citizenship or work authorization when a foreign-sounding name or education/work history does not make work
  eligibility obvious (e.g. US Citizen, US Permanent Resident).
- Prefer a modern email provider (Gmail, Outlook). Avoid AOL, Hotmail, Yahoo.
- After graduation, do not use a college email unless the school is highly prestigious (e.g. MIT, CMU).
- Do not include physical address or ZIP code.
- Do not include city/state unless applying to a role in that specific city. Non-local location can introduce bias.
- LinkedIn is unnecessary; omit it.
- Phone numbers are unnecessary; omit them. Non-local area codes can introduce bias. If a phone number must appear, do
  not label it Phone/Cell/Mobile, and for US/Canada omit the +1 country code.
- Include GitHub or portfolio links only if they have real, up-to-date content. Empty GitHub profiles must be omitted.
- Write email, GitHub, and portfolio URLs in plain text. Do not mask them as labeled hyperlinks.
- Do not preface with Email:, GitHub:, Portfolio:, etc.
- Do not underline, italicize, or color contact links.
- Do not include birthday, age, photo, or other personal details that invite bias (gender, children, nationality,
  ethnicity, marriage status, religion, political views).

## Content Strategy

- Tailor content to the target role. Prefer relevant experience over unrelated technical depth (e.g. do not lead with ML
  for a web role when stronger web experience exists).
- Include relevant technical detail and keywords so qualifications are clear and searchable, without stuffing buzzwords.
- Omit information that does not strengthen candidacy.
- No personal pronouns: I, me, we, us, my, our, their, etc.
- Differentiate via accomplishments, metrics, and technical outcomes — not job-duty lists. A resume is not a job
  description.
- Prefer engineering judgment and problem-solving over tool-centric framing. Tools are supporting detail, not the
  achievement.
- For sensitive or classified work, describe technologies and outcomes without forbidden product/program names or
  proprietary details.

## Work Experience

- Only paid work belongs here (research experience may also go here).
- Clearly label internships as internships and contract roles as contract roles.
- Order roles and bullets by relevance to the target job or by impressiveness; best content first.
- Focus on important, impressive achievements, not routine duties.
- Add context: what was done, how problems were solved, with what technical approach.
- Include technologies in bullets; technologies demonstrated in experience weigh more than a bare skills list.
- For new grads, prioritize fundamental engineering skill over management/leadership claims.
- Past roles and bullets use strong past-tense action verbs. Prefer past-tense verb openers consistently.

### Bullet Points

- Each bullet is 1–2 lines and ideally one sentence.
- Do not end bullets with periods.
- Order bullets most relevant/impressive first.
- Do not spill a bullet onto a following line with only 1–4 words.
- Do not allow hyphenated word wraps across lines.
- Avoid excess sub-bullets.
- When metrics exist, place them near the start of the bullet.
- Structure bullets with STAR, XYZ, or CAR:
    - STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result
    - XYZ: Accomplished X, measured by Y, by doing Z
    - CAR: Challenge, Action, Result
- Quantify when possible: business impact (users, cost/time saved, resource reduction), technical scale (throughput,
  dataset size, accuracy, scope of change), cross-functional scope (teams/people), delivery (on time, under budget).
- Prefer integration and system-level outcomes over parts lists. How components worked together and to what
  precision/result matters more than naming every widget.

### Action Verbs

- Start each bullet with a strong past-tense action verb.
- Good examples: analyzed, architected, automated, built, created, decreased, designed, developed, implemented,
  improved, optimized, published, reduced, refactored.
- Avoid weak verbs: aided, assisted, coded, collaborated, communicated, executed, exposed to, gained experience, helped,
  participated, programmed, ran, used, utilized, worked on.
- Avoid utilize almost always.
- Avoid superfluous or overly ornate verbs: amplified, conceptualized, crafted, elevated, employed, engaged, engineered,
  enhanced, ensured, fostered, headed, honed, innovated, mastered, orchestrated, perfected, pioneered, revolutionized,
  spearheaded, transformed.
- Be careful with frequently misused verbs such as leverage, enhance, utilize.
- Note: Led is the past tense of lead.
- Vary verbs; do not repeat the same opener across many bullets.

### Bullet content prompts

When content is thin, derive bullets from:

- What was done: problem solved, team vs independent work, team size.
- How it was done: approach, tools, standards, validation/testing and how tests changed the design.
- Results: delivery, whether it worked, what failed and what was learned.
- Whether goals were met or exceeded, and by how much.

## Skills

- Section name is Skills only.
- Purpose: highlight technical skills that match or closely relate to the job in a few words. Balance breadth with
  credibility; do not dump every technology ever touched.
- Include languages the candidate could realistically interview in.
- Include frameworks, tools, and programs actually used.
- Skills listed here should also appear in experience/project bullets where true, and vice versa for core skills.
- Do not list soft skills (teamwork, leadership, communication, Agile, Scrum, conflict resolution). Show those through
  bullets.
- Do not list assumed baseline skills: typing, Microsoft Word, IDEs, editors (VS Code, vim, LaTeX), operating systems.
- For version control: list Git or SVN, not GitHub, Bitbucket, or GitLab.
- No skill ratings or level descriptors (Expert, Professional, etc.).
- Use at most about 3 lines, single-column.
- Order skills most important to least important; drop trailing fluff.
- Categorize when helpful (e.g. Languages, Technologies, Frontend, Web frameworks, Databases, Cloud platforms, Design
  Tools).
- Separate skills with commas only (not pipes, hyphens, or dashes).
- Use correct official casing and spelling (JavaScript not Javascript or JS; SolidWorks; etc.).
- Keep functionally distinct skills separate (C, C++ not C/C++).
- Do not list many overlapping skills in the same domain (e.g. React, Angular, and Vue) unless the job needs them.
- Do not put hobbies in Skills. If a standout hobby belongs anywhere, it is a separate subsection only when nothing
  stronger fills the space.
- For early-career candidates, fewer well-supported skills beat a long list of lightly touched tools.

## Education

- Reverse chronological order (master's above bachelor's).
- Official degree names only. Write Bachelor of Science and Master of Science (not Bachelor's/Master's on the resume).
- Do not include high school.
- Do not include schools without a degree earned.
- Do not include start dates or date ranges. Graduation date only.
    - Current student: Expected May 2025
    - Graduated: May 2021 or 2021
- Do not include coursework unless extremely specialized or distinctive.
- GPA: generally include only if above 3.75 (two decimal places). After the first full-time job, remove GPA unless it is
  very impressive. Education moves toward the bottom once full-time experience exists.
- Omit school location when the name already contains it or the location is widely known.
- Omit awards and scholarships unless extremely impressive (e.g. Rhodes Scholar, Fulbright Scholar).
- Include D1 or similarly competitive sports when applicable.

## Projects

- Section name is Projects only.
- For personal projects, student design teams, and extracurricular/hobbyist work — not work projects (those belong under
  Work Experience).
- Do not use the word project in titles.
- Capitalize titles correctly.
- Roles, positions, locations, and dates are generally unnecessary for personal projects. Prefer a portfolio or repo
  link when available and valuable.
- Do not label items Personal/Academic/Group Project beside the title.
- Use bullets, not paragraphs. Same bullet rules as work experience.
- Order by relevance and impressiveness.
- Prefer real, maintained projects that solve a problem and have users (even if only the author) over mandatory
  coursework or trivial tutorials.

## Portfolios and Links

- Include GitHub or portfolio links only when current and substantive.
- Omit pinned repos without READMEs and stale portfolios.
- Strong project READMEs include summary, screenshots, how to run, and how to test, with runnable automated tests when
  possible.
- Links in plain short form without https://www.

## Senior Engineers and Above (10+ YoE)

- A brief summary (under 2 sentences) is acceptable.
- Keep separate resumes for management vs IC targets when both are in play.
- Stay at or under 2 pages.
- Include soft achievements and influence in addition to business results.
- Make earlier roles more concise.
- Place Education at the bottom.

## Career Changers

- Include a brief summary (about 2 sentences) stating the career change and motivation.
- Link to working projects and source code when available.
- Keep past non-target experience concise.
- Prefer a one-page resume.
